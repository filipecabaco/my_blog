# Product

## What this is

The personal blog of Filipe Cabaço: long-form writing about Elixir, the BEAM and
real-time systems. Posts live as markdown in the GitHub repo and are pulled and
cached at runtime; there is no CMS and no build step for content.

register: brand

## Who reads it

Backend and distributed-systems engineers, usually arriving from a link in a Slack
thread, Hacker News or Bluesky. They decide in a few seconds whether a 16-minute
article is worth their time, and if it is, they read the whole thing, including the
code.

Two things follow from that. The index has to make what a post is about legible
immediately. And the reading experience has to hold up for 3000 words of prose
mixed with Elixir.

## Tone

Direct and specific. Written by someone who had to work the thing out, not someone
selling it. No marketing register, no hedging, no exclamation.

## Principles

- **Content first.** The site opens on posts, not on a masthead. The bio lives on
  `/about`.
- **Legibility over atmosphere.** Long-form reading is the job. Anything that makes
  a post harder to read loses.
- **The real-time feature is the identity.** A process per reader, showing where
  each one is on the page, is the one thing this blog has that others do not. It
  should be visible and explained, not hidden in a corner.
- **Nothing generated by hand.** Share cards, marks and highlighting are all derived
  from the post itself, so writing a post is the only work involved in publishing.

## Anti-references

- GitHub's dark palette, which is where every developer blog lands by default.
- Monospace as costume: body copy set in a code face to signal "technical".
- Grids of identical cards that tell you nothing about which post matters.
- Analytics-vendor dashboards. The stats page is open and reads as an instrument,
  not a growth chart.
